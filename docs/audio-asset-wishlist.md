# Audio asset wishlist

Every sound in the game today is made in code (`apps/web/src/audio/sounds`) from noise, oscillators, filters, envelopes, saturation and a synthesized-impulse convolution reverb. Nothing is recorded or generated by a paid service. That keeps the download tiny (about 26 kB of code, gzipped, loaded after the page is idle; nothing plays before the first tap) and the sounds ours.

The owner's direction (19 Oct): the game must sound like war, "hard boiled and detailed … military, serious battle", not like a toy. The procedural pass gets close, and it now sounds gritty: N-wave cracks, saturated muzzle blasts, sub thumps, brass on concrete, deep blasts with debris and a ringing tail, a rough thruster, radio-style UI and a low, tense score. But **recorded or generated weapon and explosion assets are now the top priority**: real gunfire and real blasts have a density of detail that synthesis only approximates. This list ranks what to buy or generate, with prompts in that style, so the owner can decide what to spend.

Audition what's there now at `/sounds.html` (the sound board) on the playtest or `pnpm dev`. Each sound has a play button and a button per take. The bar sets the bus volumes and where a sound plays from (left or right, near or far), and runs firefight and blast stress tests.

## How a file would drop in later

1. Put it in `apps/web/public/audio/` as Opus in WebM (`.webm`), with an AAC (`.m4a`) copy for older Safari (docs/11 §6). One-shots mono, 48 kHz, peak at −1 dBFS, silence trimmed, **dry** (no baked reverb: the engine adds the space and the distance).
2. Give the sound's entry in `apps/web/src/audio/sounds/*.ts` a file (a small loader decodes it into the buffer the recipe fills today). The engine, the voice limits, the director and the board stay as they are.
3. Keep the procedural recipe as the fallback while the file loads, or if it fails.

Budget: the brief caps sound at about 1.5 MB. At Opus 64 kbps mono that's about 3 minutes of audio: every Priority 1 and 2 item below with three or four takes each fits.

A note on prompts: these are written for a sound-effects generator, or as a search brief for a licensed library. Ask for several takes of each, and for "close" and "distant" versions where the table says so (the game layers the distant one for far shooters).

## Priority 3: score and announcer

- Music bed (live today: `music.ts`, a D drone, war drums, a field snare, a clock): "Tense military underscore loop, low string and synth drones in D Phrygian, taiko and war drums marching, distant field snare, ticking, no melody, no cheerful harmony, seamless 60–90 second loop" (about 600 kB at Opus 64 kbps stereo).
- Match-start sting (`sting.start`): "Short cinematic war sting, low dissonant brass cluster and a huge war drum hit, 3 seconds, D minor, ominous".
- Results (`ui.win`, `ui.lose`): "Two heavy war drum hits and a low suspended brass swell, sombre, no fanfare" / "a single drum and a falling low brass cluster, grim".
- Announcer (none yet; the voice bus is ready): "GO", "Enemy down", "Headshot", "One minute", "Mission complete", "Mission failed", spoken by a gruff radio operator through a radio filter. A synthetic voice would sound cheap here; this is the one place a recorded (or licensed TTS) voice is worth it.

## What should stay procedural

The interface (muted switch clicks, radio blips and squelch), the countdown drum, the low-fuel and mine beeps, charge-up whines, EMP zaps, the beam and the orb hum. They are simple and tonal, and synthesis does them well.
